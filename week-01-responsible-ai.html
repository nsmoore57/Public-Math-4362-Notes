    Section Responsible AI Use in Numerical Mathematics

    AI tools may be useful as tutors, debugging assistants, and teammates, but they are not a substitute for understanding the code and mathematics you submit. When AI use is permitted, you must disclose it and check the output carefully.
    🔗
    The central rule is simple: AI may support learning when the label allows it, but it may not replace your mathematical judgment. You are responsible for being able to explain every line of code and every written answer you submit.
    🔗

    Definition 9. AI Not Permitted.

    Students must complete the marked work without AI assistance.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 10. AI Debugging Only.

    Students may use AI to understand error messages or ask guiding questions, but not to generate a full solution.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Definition 11. AI Permitted with Disclosure.

    Students may use AI as a tutor or coding assistant, but must disclose its use and understand the submitted work.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 12. A better debugging prompt.

    If a cell gives a NameError, a responsible prompt is: “Explain what this NameError means and give me questions to check. Do not write the full solution.”
    🔗
    A less responsible prompt is: “Fix my homework code and give me the final answer.” The first prompt supports learning; the second prompt tries to outsource the work.
    🔗
    🔗

    Required disclosure in submitted notebooks.

    Every submitted notebook includes an AI-use disclosure cell. Disclosure does not excuse incorrect, irrelevant, or poorly understood work. You are responsible for understanding all submitted code and explanations.
    🔗
    A useful disclosure names the tool, identifies the problem numbers where it was used, summarizes the purpose of the help, and explains what you checked or changed before submitting.

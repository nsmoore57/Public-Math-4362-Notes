    Project 11.0.1 The Gas Equation.

    The ideal gas equation relates the volume (\(V\) in L), temperature (\(T\) in K), pressure (\(P\) in atm), and the amount of gas (number of moles \(n\)) by:
    \begin{equation*} P = \frac{nRT}{V} \end{equation*}
    where \(R = 0.08206 \, (\text{L atm})/(\text{mol K})\) is the gas constant.
    🔗
    The van der Waals equation gives the relationship between these quantities for a real gas by:
    \begin{equation*} \left(P + \frac{n^2a}{V^2}\right) (V - nb) = nRT \end{equation*}
    where \(a\) and \(b\) are constants that are specific for each gas.
    🔗
    Apply an appropriate numerical method to calculate the volume of 2 mol CO₂ at a temperature of 50°C, and pressure of 6 atm. For CO₂, \(a = 3.59 \, (\text{L}^2 \text{atm})/\text{mol}^2\text{,}\) and \(b = 0.0427 \text{ L}/\text{mol}\text{.}\)

    Project 11.0.5 Scale.

    A scale is made of two springs, as shown in the figure. The springs are nonlinear such that the force they apply is given by \(F_S = K_1 u +K_2 u^3\text{,}\) where the \(K\)’s are constants and \(u=L-L_0\) is the elongation of the spring (\(L=\sqrt{a^2+(b+x)^2}\) and \(L_0=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}\) are the current and initial lengths of the springs, respectively). Initially, the springs are not stretched. When an object is attached to the ring, the springs stretch and the ring is displaced downward a distance \(x\text{.}\)
    🔗
    The weight of the object can be expressed in terms of the distance \(x\) by:
    \begin{equation*} W = 2F_S \frac{b+x}{L} \end{equation*}

    For the given scale \(a=0.22 \text{ m}\text{,}\) \(b=0.08 \text{ m}\text{,}\) and the springs’ constants are \(K_1=1600 \text{ N/m}\) and \(K_2=100000 \text{ N/m}^3\text{.}\) Plot \(W\) as a function of \(x\) for \(0 \le x \le 0.25\text{.}\) Determine the distance \(x\) when a \(400 \text{ N}\) object is attached to the scale.

    Project 11.0.8 Temperature at Equilibrium.

    Consider the problem of determining the temperature at interior points of a thin square plate, given the temperature along its edges, under the assumption that the system is in thermal equilibrium. This problem can be discretized in a straightforward way that leads directly to a system of linear equations. The key physical principle is the mean value property:
    🔗
    At equilibrium, the temperature at any interior point is equal to the average temperature of its neighboring points.
    🔗
    To solve this numerically, divide the square plate into a uniform grid of small square cells, as illustrated below for a coarse case:
    \(1\) \(1\) \(1\) \(1\) \(1\) \(1\)
    \(1\) \(x_{11}\) \(x_{12}\) \(x_{13}\) \(x_{14}\) \(0\)
    \(1\) \(x_{21}\) \(x_{22}\) \(x_{23}\) \(x_{24}\) \(0\)
    \(1\) \(x_{31}\) \(x_{32}\) \(x_{33}\) \(x_{34}\) \(0\)
    \(1\) \(x_{41}\) \(x_{42}\) \(x_{43}\) \(x_{44}\) \(0\)
    \(1\) \(0\) \(0\) \(0\) \(0\) \(0\)
    🔗
    Boundary cells (marked with numbers) have known temperatures: 1 on the top and left edges, 0 on the bottom and right edges. Interior cells (marked \(x_{ij}\)) represent unknown temperatures to be determined.
    🔗
    According to the mean value property, the temperature at each interior point equals the average of its four adjacent neighbors (up, left, down, right). This yields one equation per interior point:
    \begin{align*} x_{11} &= \frac{1}{4}(1 + 1 + x_{12} + x_{21})\\ x_{12} &= \frac{1}{4}(1 + x_{11} + x_{22} + x_{13})\\ x_{13} &= \frac{1}{4}(1 + x_{12} + x_{23} + x_{14})\\ x_{14} &= \frac{1}{4}(1 + x_{13} + x_{24} + 0)\\ x_{21} &= \frac{1}{4}(x_{11} + 1 + x_{31} + x_{22})\\ x_{22} &= \frac{1}{4}(x_{12} + x_{21} + x_{32} + x_{23})\\ x_{23} &= \frac{1}{4}(x_{13} + x_{22} + x_{33} + x_{24})\\ x_{24} &= \frac{1}{4}(x_{14} + x_{23} + x_{34} + 0)\\ x_{31} &= \frac{1}{4}(x_{21} + 1 + x_{41} + x_{32})\\ x_{32} &= \frac{1}{4}(x_{22} + x_{31} + x_{42} + x_{33})\\ x_{33} &= \frac{1}{4}(x_{23} + x_{32} + x_{43} + x_{34})\\ x_{34} &= \frac{1}{4}(x_{24} + x_{33} + x_{44} + 0)\\ x_{41} &= \frac{1}{4}(x_{31} + 1 + 0 + x_{42})\\ x_{42} &= \frac{1}{4}(x_{32} + x_{41} + 0 + x_{43})\\ x_{43} &= \frac{1}{4}(x_{33} + x_{42} + 0 + x_{44})\\ x_{44} &= \frac{1}{4}(x_{34} + x_{43} + 0 + 0) \end{align*}
    🔗
    The coarse grid above produces a system of linear equations that can be solved iteratively. After solving, the temperature field can be visualized using: 
    import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
plt.imshow(x, cmap='hot', origin='lower')
plt.colorbar()
plt.show()
    🔗
    However, results from such a coarse mesh are approximate. Finer grids provide more accurate solutions.
    🔗
    You are asked to implement a 100×100 grid to compute the equilibrium temperature distribution and generate a high-resolution heatmap of the equilibrium temperature distribution.
